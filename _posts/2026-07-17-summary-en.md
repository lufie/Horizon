## [First Atmosphere Detected on Rocky Habitable-Zone Exoplanet LHS 1140b](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cy4kdd1e0ejo) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Astronomers have detected the first atmosphere on a rocky exoplanet, LHS 1140b, which is located within the habitable zone of a red dwarf star 48 light-years away. This discovery represents a major milestone in astrobiology, proving that rocky planets orbiting within the habitable zones of active red dwarf stars can successfully retain their atmospheres, which is a key prerequisite for life. Recent data suggests LHS 1140b has a helium-rich atmosphere, and JWST emission spectroscopy has successfully ruled out the possibility of it being a gas-rich mini-Neptune, pointing instead to a rocky or water-rich composition.

hackernews · neversaydie · Jul 17, 14:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48947560)

**Background**: The habitable zone is the orbital region around a star where temperatures allow liquid water to exist on a planet's surface. While red dwarfs are the most common stars in the universe, their intense stellar flares and radiation often strip away the atmospheres of nearby planets, making the preservation of LHS 1140b's atmosphere highly unusual and scientifically valuable.

## [Kaggle Competition Sparks Debate Over AI-Driven Evaluation Inconsistencies](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggle-measuring-agi/discussion/724918#3498423) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Kaggle community discussion has exposed significant inconsistencies and potential flaws in the evaluation and judging process of the "Measuring AGI" competition. This has sparked a broader debate about how AI-generated submissions and automated AI judging are undermining the integrity of technical competitions. As AI tools become more pervasive, the reliance on automated AI judges to evaluate AI-generated code threatens the credibility of platforms like Kaggle. If participants can win through prompt injections or brute-forcing black-box models rather than genuine technical skill, the value of these benchmarks as a proving ground for real-world AI capabilities is severely diminished. Critics point out that the evaluation process lacks human oversight and common sense, leading to situations where projects can win through prompt injection or blind AI acceptance. Additionally, the discussion highlights that Kaggle's shift toward AI-assisted judging has made it easier for low-effort, fully AI-generated submissions to bypass traditional validation.

**Background**: Kaggle is a popular platform owned by Google where data scientists and machine learning practitioners compete to solve complex problems using crowdsourced benchmarks. Traditionally, these competitions evaluated submissions using objective metrics on public and private leaderboards, but recent integrations of LLMs have shifted the landscape toward generative tasks and automated evaluation.
