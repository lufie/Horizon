## [The Classic Philosophy of "Choosing Boring Technology" and Innovation Tokens](https://mcfunley.com/choose-boring-technology) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Dan McKinley's classic 2015 essay "Choose Boring Technology" continues to spark vital industry discussions, emphasizing that companies should minimize technical risk by using established technologies and reserving limited "innovation tokens" for their core business. This philosophy challenges the "best tool for the job" mentality by highlighting the long-term operational costs of adopting novel, unproven technologies. It provides engineering leaders with a practical framework to balance technical excitement with business survival. The essay argues that "boring" technologies are valuable because their failure modes are well-understood, whereas new technologies introduce unpredictable operational overhead. McKinley suggests a strict budget of about three "innovation tokens" per company to prevent teams from over-complicating their tech stacks.

hackernews · tosh · Aug 13, 17:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49289512)

**Background**: In software engineering, "boring technology" refers to well-established, widely used, and thoroughly tested tools whose bugs and limitations are well-documented. "Innovation tokens" represent a conceptual budget for taking on technical risk; spending a token on a novel technology means accepting the hidden costs of training, debugging, and maintaining an unfamiliar system.

**Discussion**: While many practitioners praise the "innovation tokens" concept as an invaluable tool for explaining trade-offs, some push back, arguing that the framework is arbitrary and that engineers should evaluate risks directly rather than relying on "newness" as a proxy. Additionally, some modern commentators suggest adapting the concept to the AI era, proposing that teams should spend their tokens on AI agents while keeping the underlying tech stack strictly "boring."
