## [Federal Judge Labels Flock License Plate Readers 'Indiscriminate Mass Surveillance'](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/federal-judge-calls-flock-indiscriminate-mass-surveillance/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A federal judge has characterized Flock Safety's automated license plate reader (ALPR) network as a form of "indiscriminate mass surveillance." This ruling marks a significant legal challenge to the widespread deployment of private-public surveillance networks. This characterization could set a major legal precedent, potentially reshaping the application of the Fourth Amendment regarding public privacy and the limits of government-backed surveillance technologies. It highlights the growing tension between public safety and civil liberties in the digital age. Flock Safety's network operates by continuously capturing and storing vehicle data, which critics argue functions as a dragnet rather than targeted law enforcement. The judge's comments bring constitutional scrutiny to how long-term data retention of non-suspects is handled.

hackernews · sbulaev · Oct 3, 22:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49948254)

**Background**: Flock Safety is a prominent technology company that provides automated license plate readers (ALPRs) to police departments and neighborhood associations across the United States. These cameras capture license plates and vehicle characteristics, creating a searchable database that helps law enforcement track vehicle movements. Traditionally, courts have held that individuals have no expectation of privacy in public spaces, but the scale and permanence of modern digital tracking are challenging this legal doctrine.

**Discussion**: Users debated the constitutionality of public tracking, with some arguing that citizens have no expectation of privacy in public, while others warned of an inevitable slide into total surveillance. Some suggested technical limits, such as only logging pre-identified suspect plates, while others pointed out the hypocrisy of citizens complaining about mass surveillance while using private tools like Ring doorbells to monitor their neighborhoods.
